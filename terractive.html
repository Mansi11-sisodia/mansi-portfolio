Terractive

Consumer research and product strategy for an active women's lifestyle brand — exploring the market, listening to real users and translating findings into product and GTM decisions.

Overview

Terractive is a project I worked on as part of MESA's Product Foundry — a structured exercise in going from a market observation to an actual product and GTM recommendation. The brand targets active women, and the work involved understanding who those women are, what they actually need and how a brand could reach them.

This is the kind of project where you discover how much space there is between a good idea and a well-reasoned recommendation. Getting from one to the other required primary research, competitive analysis and a lot of honest conversations about what the data actually showed.

Context & Problem

The active women's market is growing but crowded. There are plenty of brands competing for attention, and most of them are saying similar things. The real question isn't just "what product should we make?" — it's "who are we really for, and what do they actually want that they're not finding elsewhere?"

That question guided everything we did on this project.

My Role

I was part of the research and strategy team. My contributions included:

  • Conducting and synthesising primary consumer research
  • Mapping out the competitive landscape — who was doing what and why it was or wasn't working
  • Developing the product portfolio rationale: which SKUs to pursue and why
  • Contributing to the GTM strategy and positioning
  • Building the final pitch deck and presenting findings

Research Approach

We started with conversations — speaking to women who engaged in active lifestyles, understanding their habits, frustrations and what they were buying versus what they wished existed. We also looked at what competitors were offering and where the white space was.

Research at this stage is less about having the right answers and more about asking the right questions. We adjusted our assumptions more than once based on what we heard.

The most useful insight we came away with wasn't a data point — it was a shift in how we framed the customer. That reframe changed our product recommendations significantly.

Key Decisions & Insights

  • Who the customer actually is shapes everything. Our initial assumptions were refined significantly through conversations. The product range changed as a result.
  • Competitive analysis isn't just about what others offer. It's about understanding what customers are settling for — and why.
  • A 4-SKU portfolio with focus beats a wide range without clarity. We made a deliberate call to go narrower and stronger rather than cover too much ground.
  • GTM strategy must match who you're targeting. Where and how you reach people matters as much as what you're offering them.

Outcome & Learnings

The project concluded with a comprehensive pitch — product rationale, target audience definition, competitive positioning and a GTM plan. It was presented as part of Product Foundry's evaluation cycle.

What I took away from this project is a more structured way of thinking about product decisions — understanding that research isn't just information gathering, it's a tool for making better bets.
