Venture Operations Retail

Zyerr

A student jewellery venture — from sourcing and stall logistics to customer conversations and social media. Running a real business turned out to be less glamorous and far more interesting than it sounds.

Overview

Zyerr is a jewellery venture I worked on as a student — designed, operated and sold through physical pop-up stalls. The name says it plainly: "Any Time, Anywhere." The idea was simple: affordable, wearable jewellery that people could pick up on the spot.

This wasn't a classroom project. We set up real stalls at college events, managed real inventory and talked to real customers who had actual opinions about what they liked and what they wouldn't pay for.

Context & Opportunity

The setting was college — a place full of students who care about how they look, have a modest budget and like to find something a little different. There's a real gap between high-street jewellery that's priced for adults and cheap trinkets that feel disposable.

Zyerr was an attempt to sit in that gap: curated pieces, presented well, at a price that a student would actually pay.

My Role

I was involved across multiple parts of the operation — not just one function. My work touched:

  • Coordinating with suppliers and managing what we ordered and why
  • Setting up and running the physical stall — layout, display, signage
  • Talking directly with customers during stall hours
  • Tracking inventory and understanding what sold and what didn't
  • Contributing to how Zyerr showed up on social media
  • Supporting the day-to-day decisions that keep a small venture functioning

Running the stall taught me something no lecture could: the gap between what you think customers want and what they actually do when money changes hands.

How the Venture Operated

Zyerr operated through pop-up stalls at campus events and fairs. Each event required its own preparation — deciding what to stock, how to display it, how to price it and how to handle conversations with people who were curious but not immediately sure.

Inventory had to be sourced ahead of time from suppliers, with an eye for what was likely to move. Not everything sold, and figuring out why something didn't sell was often as instructive as figuring out why something did.

The stall setup mattered more than I initially expected. A well-displayed item got picked up. A poorly placed one got ignored even if it was better.

Key Decisions & Learnings

  • Customer conversations are the best research. Listening to what people said — and noticed what they didn't say — told us more about product-market fit than any survey would have.
  • Display is a decision, not a detail. How things look on the table affects what people pick up and what they buy. We adjusted mid-stall more than once.
  • Operations determine everything. Having the right items, in the right quantities, at the right time — that's what makes or breaks a stall day.
  • Social media is a continuous commitment. Building familiarity with an audience takes consistency, not just a few posts.
  • Learning from what didn't work is the most underrated skill. Not every item moved. That data mattered.

Outcome & What I Took Away

Zyerr gave me hands-on experience with the full cycle of a small retail venture — sourcing, operations, customer interaction, inventory decisions and brand presence. It's the kind of experience that changes how you think about the mechanics of a business.

More than any specific outcome, I came away with a clearer instinct for what actually matters when you're operating something in the real world — and a genuine respect for how much work goes into making something look effortless.
